Abstract. Let Mh(ξ)\mathrm{Mh}(\xi) be the ξ\xi-th iterate of the recursively Mahlo operation along the ordinals: Mh(ξ+1)\mathrm{Mh}(\xi+1) is the class of ordinals that are Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on Mh(ξ)\mathrm{Mh}(\xi), with intersections at limits. For an admissible ordinal α>ω\alpha > \omega this iteration is defined at degree α\alpha, but α∉Mh(α+1)\alpha \notin \mathrm{Mh}(\alpha+1). We study higher degrees through presentations: definable relations on LαL_{\alpha}, evaluated at smaller levels. Call α\alpha α+\alpha^+-recursively Mahlo (RM+) if it lies in the Mahlo class of every node in the externally well-founded part of each Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation at α\alpha. We show that the least RM+ ordinal is the least Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinal KK, and that an admissible ordinal up to a certain bound is RM+ exactly when it is Π3\Pi_3-reflecting or Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on the Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinals. Hence the first RM+ ordinal that is not Π3\Pi_3-reflecting is the least ordinal γ1\gamma_1 that is Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on the Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinals. Restricting to Σ1\Sigma_1 linear orderings gives the same notion.

1. Introduction

A cardinal κ\kappa is greatly Mahlo if it is Mahlo of every degree below κ+\kappa^+, the degrees at or above κ\kappa being defined through canonical functions (Baumgartner, Taylor and Wagon). Below a weakly compact cardinal there are stationarily many greatly Mahlo cardinals.

In the recursive setting, regular cardinals are replaced by admissible ordinals, stationary reflection by Π2\Pi_2-reflection on a class, and weak compactness by Π3\Pi_3-reflection (Richter and Aczel). The ordinal analysis of Π3\Pi_3-reflection is due to Rathjen. Arai iterates the recursively Mahlo operation along a definable relation that is evaluated in the current universe (Iterating, Definition 1.1), and shows that a Π3\Pi_3-reflecting universe lies in the class of every n∈ωn \in \omega up to which transfinite induction along a Σ1\Sigma_1 relation on ω\omega holds (Lemma 1.2; the same step is Lemma 3.33 of his Lectures, where Remark 3.34 notes that it is the only use of Π3\Pi_3-reflection). His main theorem is proof-theoretic: the theory of Π3\Pi_3-reflecting universes is reducible to the theory of limits of admissibles together with such iterations along one particular Σ1\Sigma_1 relation (Theorem 1.3). Theorem A below is the same argument for Σ1\Sigma_1 presentations with parameters (Section 3.2), with the well-founded part taken externally and with the strict reflection convention of Section 2. This note asks the set-theoretic question that corresponds to Arai’s reduction: which is the least ordinal in all these classes, and which is the first one that is not Π3\Pi_3-reflecting. We did not find Theorems B and C or Proposition D in the sources we read; see Section 10.

Throughout, 𝑨\boldsymbol{A} is the class of Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinals, 𝑲\boldsymbol{K} =min⁡A= \min\, A, and 𝜸𝟏\boldsymbol{\gamma_1} is the least ordinal that is Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on AA. RM+ is defined in 3.3.

Theorem A. Every Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinal is RM+.

Theorem B. No ordinal below KK is RM+. Hence the least RM+ ordinal is KK.

Theorem C. An admissible ordinal γ\gamma with ω<γ≤γ1\omega < \gamma \le \gamma_1 is RM+ if and only if γ∈A\gamma \in A or γ\gamma is Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on AA. Hence the least RM+ ordinal that is not Π3\Pi_3-reflecting is γ1\gamma_1.

Proposition D. For admissible α>ω\alpha > \omega, the definition of RM+ is unchanged if only Σ1\Sigma_1 presentations that define linear orderings of LαL_{\alpha} are allowed.

So RM+ and Π3\Pi_3-reflection have the same least element but are different classes.

Overview of the proofs. Why α+\alpha^+. A node in the well-founded part of a Σ1\Sigma_1 relation on LαL_{\alpha} has rank below α+\alpha^+. The ranks that occur are exactly the order types of well-orderings that are Δ1\Delta_1-definable over LαL_{\alpha}, and at every admissible α\alpha below the least Σ11\Sigma^1_1-reflecting ordinal these fill all of α+\alpha^+ (remark after Definition 3.3). RM+ asks for membership in the Mahlo class of every such node, as a greatly Mahlo cardinal κ\kappa is Mahlo of every degree below κ+\kappa^+. The class of a node is not fixed by its rank: the relation is evaluated again at each smaller level (Definition 3.1, Examples 3.2).

The upper bound. At an admissible level, membership in the class of a node is expressed by a Π3\Pi_3 sentence (Lemma 5.1). A Π3\Pi_3-reflecting κ\kappa therefore reflects “φ\varphi, and the current level is in the class of cc” for each predecessor cc of a node and each Π2\Pi_2 sentence φ\varphi true in LκL_{\kappa}, and induction on the rank of the node gives Theorem A. This is Arai’s argument.

The lower bound. Fix a Π2\Pi_2 sentence φ0(q0)\varphi_0(q_0). Section 6 defines a Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation TT, a tree whose nodes are finite increasing sequences of levels, each enumerated by ω\omega in the next. Each step handles one task: it supplies a witness for φ0(q0)\varphi_0(q_0), or it decides one Π3\Pi_3 sentence ∀u∃v∀wθ\forall u\, \exists v\, \forall w\, \theta with constants already enumerated, either positively, by naming an earlier limit level in which it holds, or negatively, by naming uu and promising ∀v∃w¬θ\forall v\, \exists w\, \neg\theta; later steps supply the witnesses ww that keep the promise. An infinite branch builds a Π3\Pi_3-reflecting level satisfying φ0(q0)\varphi_0(q_0) (Lemma 6.1). Below KK, with φ0\varphi_0 trivial, there is no such level, so the root is in the well-founded part; ordinals that are not admissible are excluded by Example 3.2(1). To see that γ\gamma is not in the class of the root, follow nodes whose promises are true in LγL_{\gamma}. A promise is Π2\Pi_2, so “this node’s promises are true” is a Π2\Pi_2 sentence that reflects down together with the node, and the descent lemma (Lemma 4.1) turns this into non-membership in the class (Theorem M). The only obstacle to extending such a node is a bad task: a Π3\Pi_3 sentence true in LγL_{\gamma} and in no earlier limit level, which can be decided neither positively nor by a true promise. Such a sentence is false at every smaller level containing its constant, so it cannot block a second time further down (Lemma 6.3). The tree enumerates each earlier level by ω\omega, and Lemma 7.2 shows that LγL_{\gamma} can do this for every admissible γ\gamma in the range of Theorems B and C.

Theorem C. The same tree is used with φ0(q0)\varphi_0(q_0) a Π2\Pi_2 sentence that witnesses the failure of Π2\Pi_2-reflection on AA (Lemma 7.3). The converse direction applies Theorem A at the elements of AA to which sentences are reflected (Lemma 7.4). Lemma 7.1 shows that γ1\gamma_1 exists and Lemma 7.5 that it is not Π3\Pi_3-reflecting.

Proposition D. A Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation is replaced by the Kleene–Brouwer ordering of its finite descending sequences, recorded with edge witnesses; this does not need Sections 5–7.

What to scrutinise. RM+ is a statement about presentations: a definable relation is evaluated at every smaller level, and its class depends on that evaluation and not only on its rank. RM+ is therefore a presentation-based iteration of recursive Mahloness. We do not prove that it corresponds to a recursive form of the canonical-function or normal-ideal definition of greatly Mahlo cardinals, and the name “recursively greatly Mahlo” should be read with that reservation (Question 3 of Section 9). In particular Theorem B does not conflict with the stationary-set theorem for cardinals quoted above: its proof enumerates each earlier level by ω\omega inside the ambient level, which is impossible at an inaccessible cardinal, and the cardinal notion evaluates degrees through canonical functions modulo the nonstationary ideal, which is a different evaluation from Definition 3.1. The notion is also sensitive to the complexity of the presentations (Section 3.4).

2. Preliminaries

Background. The following are standard and used without proof: Δ0\Delta_0 absoluteness; Kripke–Platek set theory, of which every admissible level is a model, and its Σ\Sigma-recursion theorem (Barwise, Theorem I.6.4); that the map ν↦Lν\nu \mapsto L_{\nu} and the satisfaction relation of LνL_{\nu} are Σ1\Sigma_1-definable, and so Δ1\Delta_1-definable, over every admissible set (Devlin, Lemma I.9.10 and Section II.2); the condensation lemma (Devlin, Theorem II.5.2); and Richter and Aczel’s characterisation of the Πn\Pi_n-reflecting ordinals, of which we use Theorem 1.8, recalled below.

One point needs care. Formula complexity is counted below in the strict prenex sense, and no collection principle is used to rearrange quantifiers, because the levels to which sentences are reflected need not be admissible. The usual Σ1\Sigma_1 definitions of LνL_{\nu} and of its satisfaction relation are justified by Σ\Sigma-recursion, so at admissible levels. We therefore fix one explicit Σ1\Sigma_1 form of them: formulas asserting the existence of a certificate, a set that records the levels up to an ordinal with their satisfaction relations and is checked by a Δ0\Delta_0 formula. Certificates are correct wherever they exist, and a fixed Π2\Pi_2 sentence GG asserts that they exist for every ordinal; so they can be used at every limit level satisfying GG, not only at admissible ones. This is the standard construction of LL inside Kripke–Platek set theory, set up so that the quantifier prefix of every formula used later can be read off. The verifications are given in full.

LαL_{\alpha} is the α\alpha-th level of the constructible hierarchy. Formulas are in the language of set theory with a constant for each set; a sentence is evaluated in LβL_{\beta} only if its constants are elements of LβL_{\beta}.

Levels. A level is a set LνL_{\nu}; it is transitive, its ordinals are the ordinals below ν\nu, and the earlier levels are those of smaller index. A set is countable in LγL_{\gamma} if it is empty or the image of a surjection from ω\omega that belongs to LγL_{\gamma}. A limit level, or an admissible level, is LνL_{\nu} with ν\nu a limit, or an admissible, ordinal.

Formula classes. A formula is Δ0\Delta_0 if all its quantifiers are bounded. It is Πn\Pi_n if it is logically equivalent to a formula ∀x1∃x2⋯θ\forall x_1\, \exists x_2 \cdots \theta with nn alternating blocks of unbounded quantifiers and θ∈Δ0\theta \in \Delta_0; Σn\Sigma_n is dual. This is the strict prenex convention of Richter and Aczel. No closure under bounded quantification is assumed, and no collection principle is used to rearrange quantifiers.

Reflection. α\alpha is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting if every Πn\Pi_n sentence φ(q)\varphi(q) with q∈Lαq \in L_{\alpha} that is true in LαL_{\alpha} is true in LβL_{\beta} for some β<α\beta < \alpha with q∈Lβq \in L_{\beta}. For a class XX of ordinals, μ\mu is Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on XX if every Π2\Pi_2 sentence φ(q)\varphi(q) with q∈Lμq \in L_{\mu} that is true in LμL_{\mu} is true in LβL_{\beta} for some β∈X∩μ\beta \in X \cap \mu with q∈Lβq \in L_{\beta}. A limit ordinal α>ω\alpha > \omega is Π2\Pi_2-reflecting if and only if it is admissible (Richter–Aczel, Theorem 1.8). Convention: the ordinals called Πn\Pi_n-reflecting (n≥2n \ge 2) or Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on a class are limit ordinals above ω\omega. Tests include parameter-free sentences and sentences with any finite list of set parameters, each of which must be an element of LβL_{\beta} at the target β\beta. The target need not be admissible.

Absoluteness and recursion. Two standard facts are used throughout (Barwise, Corollary I.8.6 and Theorem I.6.4). Δ0\Delta_0 absoluteness: if M⊆NM \subseteq N are transitive sets, or NN is the universe, θ\theta is Δ0\Delta_0 and x1,…,xn∈Mx_1, \dots, x_n \in M, then M⊨θ(x1,…,xn)M \models \theta(x_1, \dots, x_n) if and only if N⊨θ(x1,…,xn)N \models \theta(x_1, \dots, x_n). A condition on given sets is called bounded if it is expressed by a Δ0\Delta_0 formula with those sets as constants; by absoluteness it does not matter in which transitive set containing them it is evaluated. Σ\Sigma-recursion: an admissible level is a model of Kripke–Platek set theory, and there a Σ1\Sigma_1-definable recursion step that gives a unique next set for each ordinal and each set-coded earlier history defines a Σ1\Sigma_1-definable function by recursion on the ordinals, whose restriction to each ordinal of the level is a set (the Σ\Sigma-recursion theorem).

Certificates. Formulas without constants are coded by hereditarily finite sets, each code carrying a declared finite context of free variables, and a sentence ψ(q)\psi(q) is handled as the code of ψ\psi together with the value qq of its one variable. The syntax data are ω\omega and the sets of codes of formulas built in at most nn steps, n∈ωn \in \omega, with the bounded formulas and the prenex formulas ∀x∃yθ\forall x\, \exists y\, \theta and ∀u∃v∀wθ\forall u\, \exists v\, \forall w\, \theta with bounded θ\theta marked. A certificate through ξ\xi is a package WW of syntax data and a function on ξ+1\xi+1 whose values are triples W(ν)=(Mν,Fν,Trν)W(\nu) = (M_\nu, F_\nu, \mathrm{Tr}_\nu), the candidate level, assignment domain and truth table at ν\nu, subject to bounded conditions: (V1) FνF_\nu is a set of finite tuples from MνM_\nu that contains the empty tuple and is closed under one-place extension by elements of MνM_\nu; (V2) Trν\mathrm{Tr}_\nu satisfies the Tarski clauses over MνM_\nu; (V3) M0=∅M_0 = \emptyset, MνM_\nu is the union of the earlier MηM_\eta at limits, and Mν+1M_{\nu+1} consists exactly of the subsets of MνM_\nu defined by a formula code and an assignment from FνF_\nu according to Trν\mathrm{Tr}_\nu. Write Cert(W,ξ)\mathrm{Cert}(W, \xi) for the conjunction of these conditions with those on the syntax data; it is a Δ0\Delta_0 formula. The true certificate through ξ\xi is the one in which, for every ν≤ξ\nu \le \xi, MνM_\nu is LνL_{\nu}, FνF_\nu is the set of all finite tuples from LνL_{\nu} and Trν\mathrm{Tr}_\nu is the satisfaction relation of LνL_{\nu}.

The definition in full.

Fix a coding of the formulas without constants by hereditarily finite sets, in which each formula code carries a declared finite context of free variables; unused variables are allowed. A sentence ψ(q)\psi(q) with a constant is handled as the code of ψ\psi, with a one-variable context, together with the value qq of that variable. The syntax data are ω\omega and, for each n∈ωn \in \omega, the set of codes of formulas built in at most nn steps, each with its context, with the bounded formulas and the prenex formulas ∀x∃yθ\forall x\, \exists y\, \theta and ∀u∃v∀wθ\forall u\, \exists v\, \forall w\, \theta with bounded θ\theta marked among them. That a set is ω\omega, that the first of these sets is the atomic base, and that each of them arises from the previous one by exactly one formation step, with the contexts and marks as indicated, are bounded conditions checked in both directions, and by induction on nn they are satisfied only by the true syntax data.

  • (V1) For each ν≤ξ\nu \le \xi: every member of FνF_\nu is a finite tuple from MνM_\nu, the empty tuple belongs to FνF_\nu, and every one-place extension of a member of FνF_\nu by an element of MνM_\nu belongs to FνF_\nu.

  • (V2) For each ν≤ξ\nu \le \xi: Trν\mathrm{Tr}_\nu is a set of pairs (φ,s)(\varphi, s) of a formula code and a tuple s∈Fνs \in F_\nu whose length is that of the context of φ\varphi. Quantifier blocks. In a limit level, a block of like quantifiers can be contracted to one quantifier over finite tuples, using bounded quantifiers only. We write Π3\Pi_3 sentences as ∀u∃v∀wθ\forall u\, \exists v\, \forall w\, \theta, and we only ever use this contraction at limit levels. 3. Presentations and the notion RM+

3.1 Iterating along the ordinals

Let Mh(0)\mathrm{Mh}(0) be the class of limit ordinals above ω\omega, Mh(ξ+1)\mathrm{Mh}(\xi+1) the class of ordinals that are Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on Mh(ξ)\mathrm{Mh}(\xi), and Mh(λ)\mathrm{Mh}(\lambda) the intersection of the earlier classes for limit λ\lambda. The operation of Π2\Pi_2-reflection on a class is monotone under inclusion, and its first iterate here is contained in Mh(0)\mathrm{Mh}(0); so by induction, taking intersections at limits, Mh(ζ)⊆Mh(ξ)\mathrm{Mh}(\zeta) \subseteq \mathrm{Mh}(\xi) for ξ≤ζ\xi \le \zeta. Then Mh(1)\mathrm{Mh}(1) is the class of admissible ordinals above ω\omega and Mh(2)\mathrm{Mh}(2) the class of recursively Mahlo ordinals. Here Mh(ξ)\mathrm{Mh}(\xi) depends only on the ordinal ξ\xi, not on a relation that presents it. This iteration is defined for all ξ\xi, but no μ\mu is in Mh(μ+1)\mathrm{Mh}(\mu+1): if μ\mu were the least such ordinal, it would be Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on Mh(μ)\mathrm{Mh}(\mu), giving β<μ\beta < \mu in Mh(μ)⊆Mh(β+1)\mathrm{Mh}(\mu) \subseteq \mathrm{Mh}(\beta+1). To express higher degrees at μ\mu one has to say how a degree is evaluated at smaller levels.

3.2 Presentations and their classes

A presentation is a pair P=(ρ,p)P = (\rho, p) of a formula ρ(x,y,z)\rho(x, y, z) and a set pp. At a limit ordinal μ>ω\mu > \omega with p∈Lμp \in L_{\mu} it defines a relation on LμL_{\mu}: cc is a predecessor of aa at μ\mu if Lμ⊨ρ(c,a,p)L_{\mu} \models \rho(c, a, p), and then (c,a)(c, a) is an edge at μ\mu. Elements of LμL_{\mu} are called nodes. PP is a Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation if ρ\rho is Σ1\Sigma_1.

Definition 3.1. The class C(P,a)C(P, a) of a node aa is defined by recursion on μ\mu, for all nodes simultaneously:

μ∈C(P,a)\mu \in C(P, a) if and only if μ\mu is a limit ordinal above ω\omega, pp and aa are elements of LμL_{\mu}, and μ\mu is Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on C(P,c)C(P, c) for every predecessor cc of aa at μ\mu.

The right-hand side refers only to C(P,c)∩μC(P, c) \cap \mu. The relation is not required to be well-founded. A descending sequence from a node aa is a sequence a=a0,a1,a2,…a = a_0, a_1, a_2, \dots in which each an+1a_{n+1} is a predecessor of ana_n. If there is no infinite descending sequence from aa, the rank of aa is the ordinal defined by recursion as the supremum of the ordinals (rank of cc)+1\,+\,1 over the predecessors cc of aa. Both notions refer to the relation at a given ordinal μ\mu.

Examples 3.2. (1) If rr is a well-founded relation that is a set and ρ\rho says (x,y)∈z(x, y) \in z with p=rp = r, then C(P,a)C(P, a) is Mh(\mathrm{Mh}(rank of a)a) restricted to the μ\mu with r,a∈Lμr, a \in L_{\mu}. 3.3 The notion

Definition 3.3. A limit ordinal α>ω\alpha > \omega is α+\alpha^+-recursively Mahlo (RM+) if α∈C(P,a)\alpha \in C(P, a) for every Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation PP with p∈Lαp \in L_{\alpha} and every node a∈Lαa \in L_{\alpha} in the well-founded part of the relation defined by PP at α\alpha. Here the well-founded part of a relation is the set of nodes below which it has no infinite descending sequence, taken in the universe and not inside LαL_{\alpha}.

By Example 3.2(1) every RM+ ordinal is admissible. For admissible α\alpha let δα\delta_{\alpha} be the supremum of the order types of well-orderings with field contained in LαL_{\alpha} that are Δ1\Delta_1-definable over LαL_{\alpha} with parameters. A node in the well-founded part at α\alpha has rank below δα\delta_{\alpha}: the ordering QQ of Section 8, built from the node’s histories, is such a well-ordering, and the rank of the node is at most the order type of the histories that come before the empty history. Conversely, every ordinal below δα\delta_{\alpha} is the rank of a node, since a Δ1\Delta_1 well-ordering is itself a Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation. Always δα≤α+\delta_{\alpha} \le \alpha^+, the next admissible. By a theorem of Gostanian (see Aguilera, Section 2), δα=α+\delta_{\alpha} = \alpha^+ for every countable admissible α\alpha below the least Σ11\Sigma^1_1-reflecting ordinal, and not at that ordinal. That ordinal is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting for every nn, so by Lemmas 7.1 and 7.5 it lies above γ1\gamma_1; hence δα=α+\delta_{\alpha} = \alpha^+ throughout the range of Theorems B and C. At larger admissible α\alpha the definition measures degrees below δα\delta_{\alpha}, which can be smaller than α+\alpha^+. The rank of a node bounds what the node presents; it does not determine its class.

3.4 Persistence and the complexity of presentations

By (C5), edges of a Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation at μ\mu are edges at every γ>μ\gamma > \mu. So a node in the well-founded part at γ\gamma is in the well-founded part at every smaller μ\mu with the node in LμL_{\mu}. For a Π2\Pi_2 presentation this fails. Two statements about other complexity classes are not proved in this paper and are not used in it: that the analogue of RM+ for Π2\Pi_2 presentations is equivalent to Π3\Pi_3-reflection (a Π3\Pi_3 sentence that fails to reflect can be used to construct an obstruction of rank 2), and that Theorem A fails for Σ4\Sigma_4 presentations.

8. Linear orderings

The canonical well-ordering. The canonical well-ordering of LL, in which each level is an initial segment, is Σ1\Sigma_1-definable, and so Δ1\Delta_1-definable, over admissible levels (Devlin, Lemma II.3.3). As with certificates, we fix one form of this definition. An order certificate through ξ\xi is a certificate WW through ξ\xi together with relations <ν<_\nu on MνM_\nu, ν≤ξ\nu \le \xi, subject to bounded clauses that follow the construction of the order: <0<_0 is empty, unions are taken at limits, and at a successor the elements of MνM_\nu come first, in the order <ν<_\nu, followed by the new elements, ordered by their least definitions. The relations of any order certificate are the restrictions to the levels of the canonical well-ordering, and an admissible LαL_{\alpha} contains order certificates through every ξ<α\xi < \alpha. So the existence of an order certificate giving a comparison is Σ1\Sigma_1, it gives the true comparison when present, and in LαL_{\alpha} it is present, in one direction, for any two distinct sets.

Self-contained proof.

Augment certificates by the constructible orders: an order certificate through ξ\xi is a certificate WW through ξ\xi (Section 2) together with a relation <ν<_\nu on MνM_\nu for each ν≤ξ\nu \le \xi, such that <0<_0 is empty, unions are taken at limits, and for ν<ξ\nu < \xi and x,y∈Mν+1x, y \in M_{\nu+1}: x<ν+1yx <_{\nu+1} y if and only if either x,y∈Mνx, y \in M_\nu and x<νyx <_\nu y, or x∈Mνx \in M_\nu and y∉Mνy \notin M_\nu, or x,y∉Mνx, y \notin M_\nu and the least defining pair of xx precedes that of yy. Here a pair (φ,s)(\varphi, s) of a formula code and a tuple s∈Fνs \in F_\nu defines yy if it yields yy in the sense of (V3), and pairs are ordered by formula number and then by the previous order on tuples, compared by length and then lexicographically by <ν<_\nu. The numbering of the formula codes is a fixed primitive recursive one. An order certificate carries it as part of its syntax data, together with the graphs of the arithmetic functions used to define it, as total functions on the natural numbers; their base and recursion equations are bounded conditions and, as for the rest of the syntax data, only the true graphs satisfy them. Defining pairs use the assignment convention of (V3). These conditions are bounded on the sets of the certificate, since “least defining pair” quantifies only over formula codes and members of FνF_\nu. As in (C2), an admissible LαL_{\alpha} contains order certificates through every ξ<α\xi < \alpha, by Σ\Sigma-recursion. So the existence of an order certificate giving a comparison is Σ1\Sigma_1, it gives the true comparison when present, and in LαL_{\alpha} it is present, in one direction, for any two distinct sets. ◻

Proposition D. Let α>ω\alpha > \omega be admissible. Then α\alpha is RM+ if and only if α∈C(Q,x)\alpha \in C(Q, x) for every Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation QQ that defines a linear ordering of LαL_{\alpha} at α\alpha and every node xx in the well-founded part of that ordering.

Proof.

One direction is a restriction of the quantifier. For the other, let P=(ρ,p)P = (\rho, p) be a Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation, with ρ(c,b,p)\rho(c, b, p) of the form ∃wθ(c,b,p,w)\exists w\, \theta(c, b, p, w), θ∈Δ0\theta \in \Delta_0, and let aa be a node in the well-founded part at α\alpha. With the parameter (p,a,ω)(p, a, \omega), being a history is a Δ0\Delta_0 property, because the edge witnesses are recorded. Define QQ, with parameter (p,a,ω)(p, a, \omega), as follows: histories come before all other sets; between histories, a proper extension comes before the history it extends, and otherwise histories are compared at the first place where they differ, by the canonical well-ordering of LL; sets that are not histories are compared by the canonical well-ordering of LL.

The canonical well-ordering is Σ1\Sigma_1, through the order certificates described before the proposition.

The formula defining QQ is the disjunction of the cases listed above. In each case the conditions on histories are Δ0\Delta_0 and a comparison by the canonical well-ordering is Σ1\Sigma_1; the proper-extension clause for two histories needs no order certificate. So QQ is a Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation, and at the admissible α\alpha it linearly orders LαL_{\alpha}.

The empty history is in the well-founded part of QQ at α\alpha. An infinite descending sequence of histories in this ordering yields an infinite branch through the tree of histories, hence an infinite descending sequence from aa in the relation defined by PP at α\alpha; there is none. (This is the usual fact that the Kleene–Brouwer ordering of a well-founded tree over a well-ordered alphabet is a well-ordering.) Every nonempty history lies below the empty history, and all sets that are not histories lie above it. In fact the whole ordering at α\alpha is a well-ordering, so the proposition also holds with “well-ordering” in place of “linear ordering”.

Only the implication (*)(\ast) is proved or needed; a linear ordering has more predecessors than the tree and its classes can be smaller. The construction does not make the ordering at a smaller level an initial segment of the ordering at a larger one, and it does not choose one canonical presentation for each order type. A notion with such coherence requirements would be a different notion, to which this proposition does not apply.

9. Remarks and questions

  1. The method stops at Π3\Pi_3. In the analogous tree for Π4\Pi_4-reflection, the promise left by a negative answer is Π3\Pi_3, so corr(s)\mathrm{corr}(s) is Π3\Pi_3 and τ\tau is no longer Π2\Pi_2; the classes of Definition 3.1 only reflect Π2\Pi_2 sentences. The argument must fail there: KK is RM+ but is not Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on the Π4\Pi_4-reflecting ordinals.

  2. A related result. Richter and Aczel show that the supremum of the closure ordinals of effective Π20\Pi^0_2 operators on subsets of ω\omega (non-monotone operators allowed, with accumulating iteration) is KK. We expect a second proof of the lower bound in Theorem B from a tree that guesses the closed stage of a universal Π20\Pi^0_2 inductive definition, but we have not written it out. The proof given here uses Theorem M.

  3. Question. Is RM+ equivalent to a recursive form of the canonical-function or normal-ideal definition of greatly Mahlo cardinals, with suitable coherence requirements on the presentations? (See the end of Section 8.) For cardinals, Arai relates his Mahlo classes of degree σ+\sigma^+ to the greatly Mahlo cardinals of Baumgartner, Taylor and Wagon (Proof theory of weak compactness, Proposition 2.10); that would be the place to start.

  4. Question. Theorem C assumes that every LaL_a with a<γa < \gamma is countable in LγL_{\gamma}, which fails at sufficiently large admissible γ\gamma. What is RM+ there?

  5. Question. One level down, with “admissible limit of” in place of “Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on” and recursively Mahlo in place of Π3\Pi_3-reflecting, the analogous statements appear to hold by the analogous argument. We have only sketched this. The provision “limit of limit ordinals” used for making room in Lemma 6.2 has no analogue two levels down.

  6. Question. By the remark after Definition 3.3, RM+ only concerns degrees below α+\alpha^+. Nothing in this paper addresses degrees from α+\alpha^+ on. They would need presentations defined over levels above LαL_{\alpha}, and we do not know whether a natural notion of that kind matches a stronger reflection property or a stability property.
