Abstract. Earlier work defined degrees of recursive inaccessibility by iterating “admissible limit of” along the well-founded nodes of Σ1\Sigma_1-definable relations, read afresh at each constructible level, and then along finite exponents and the first limit exponent ω\omega. Here a well-founded node of any Σ1\Sigma_1 relation may serve as the exponent: a limit γ>ω\gamma > \omega has degree δγδγ\delta_\gamma^{\delta_\gamma} if it lies in the iterated class for every such exponent. We show that, at or below the least ordinal ξ*\xi^* that is Πm\Pi_m-reflecting for every mm, the admissible ordinals of degree δγδγ\delta_\gamma^{\delta_\gamma} are exactly the Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinals and their admissible limits; so the least such ordinal is the least Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinal. Along recursive well-orderings, degree δξ+1\delta^{\xi+1} corresponds to recursive ξ\xi-Mahloness, where the recursively (ξ+1)(\xi+1)-Mahlo ordinals are those Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on the recursively ξ\xi-Mahlo ones; at a limit λ\lambda, the least ordinal of degree δλ\delta^\lambda lies strictly below the least recursively λ\lambda-Mahlo ordinal.

Provenance. The proofs were found and written by AI systems. No human specialist has reviewed them, nothing is formalised in Lean, and novelty is not established. Section 8 states what has been checked.

1. Introduction

We build on three earlier papers of ours, listed in the references: [A] on Πn\Pi_n-reflection of degree δα\delta_\alpha, [B] on recursive inaccessibility of degree δαk+1\delta_\alpha^{k+1}, and [C] on the least α+\alpha^+-recursively Mahlo ordinal. Items are cited as [B, Lemma 4.2].

A Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation is a Σ1\Sigma_1 formula with a parameter, read at each level LμL_\mu as a binary relation on LμL_\mu; well-foundedness is taken in the universe. [A] calls a limit α>ω\alpha > \omega recursively inaccessible of degree δα\delta_\alpha if it lies in every class obtained by iterating “admissible limit of” along a well-founded node of a Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation at α\alpha; here δα\delta_\alpha is the supremum of the order types of well-orderings Δ1\Delta_1-definable over LαL_\alpha with parameters, and for admissible α\alpha these are the ranks of such nodes [A, Theorem 3.1]. [B] iterates the passage to this degree along the ordinals k≤ωk \le \omega. Here a node aa of any Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation PP supplies the exponent: over a base class XX, the class X⟨a⟩PX\langle a\rangle_P asks, for every predecessor cc of aa and every well-founded node ee of every Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation QQ, for the iteration of Y↦Y⟨c⟩PY \mapsto Y\langle c\rangle_P along ee (Definition 3.1). A limit α>ω\alpha > \omega has degree δαδα\delta_\alpha^{\delta_\alpha} if it lies in Ad⟨a⟩P\mathrm{Ad}\langle a\rangle_P for every such pair (P,a)(P,a), and degree δαζ\delta_\alpha^\zeta if it lies in Ad⟨a⟩P\mathrm{Ad}\langle a\rangle_P for a node aa at position ζ≥1\zeta \ge 1 of a recursive well-ordering (Definition 3.3; by Lemma 3.4 the well-ordering does not matter).

The main result is the following.

Theorem 6.9 (main theorem). An admissible γ≤ξ*\gamma \le \xi^* is recursively inaccessible of degree δγδγ\delta_\gamma^{\delta_\gamma} if and only if γ\gamma is Π3\Pi_3-reflecting or a limit of Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinals. The least ordinal α\alpha of degree δαδα\delta_\alpha^{\delta_\alpha} is the least Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinal K2K_2.

This answers the third question of [A, Section 6] and the first question of [B, Section 8]. Arai relates the theory of Π3\Pi_3-reflecting universes to iterations of the recursively Mahlo operation M2M_2 (Iterating the recursively Mahlo operations, Theorem 1.3, i=2i = 2), and Rathjen’s analysis uses related Mahlo classes of cardinals (Proof theory of reflection, Remark 4.9 of the 1993 preprint); see [B, Section 1] for the comparison. Here the basic operation is “admissible limit of”. By [A, Theorem 5.8], iterating it along every eligible node gives the least recursively Mahlo ordinal; Theorem 6.9 lets eligible nodes supply the outer exponent as well, and identifies the least ordinal of the resulting degree with K2K_2.

We call the members of Mh(2+ξ)\mathrm{Mh}(2+\xi) recursively ξ\xi-Mahlo, extending the usage of [A] and [B] (Section 2.3). Along recursive well-orderings the degrees are the following.

Theorem 5.6 (recursive exponents). Let γ≤ξ*\gamma \le \xi^* be admissible and aa a node of a recursive well-ordering.

  • If |a|=0|a| = 0, then γ∈Ad[a]\gamma \in \mathrm{Ad}[a] if and only if γ\gamma is recursively inaccessible.

  • If |a|=ξ+1|a| = \xi+1, then γ\gamma has degree δγξ+1\delta_\gamma^{\xi+1} if and only if γ\gamma is recursively ξ\xi-Mahlo or a limit of recursively ξ\xi-Mahlo ordinals.

  • If |a|=λ|a| = \lambda is a limit, then γ\gamma has degree δγλ\delta_\gamma^\lambda if and only if γ\gamma is a limit of recursively ξ\xi-Mahlo ordinals for every ξ<λ\xi < \lambda.

By Corollary 5.7 the least ordinal of degree δλ\delta^\lambda lies below the least recursively λ\lambda-Mahlo ordinal, so the pattern “degree δξ\delta^\xi has the least element of Mh(1+ξ)\mathrm{Mh}(1+\xi)”, noted in [B, Section 8] to fail at ω\omega, fails at every infinite recursive ξ\xi; among recursive exponents, ω+1\omega+1 is the only one whose least element is the least recursively ω\omega-Mahlo ordinal.

Sufficiency is the reflection argument of [B, Theorem 5.2] (Theorems 5.1, 6.3). Necessity is a least-counterexample descent along a Σ1\Sigma_1 search tree as in [B, Theorem 6.5] (Theorem 5.4). The new idea is in the case of all presentations (Theorem 6.8). A node aa of PP is split into two markers a−≺a+a^- \prec a^+, and every predecessor dd of aa gives d+≺a−d^+ \prec a^- (Lemma 6.6). Membership in Y⟨a−⟩Y\langle a^-\rangle then implies membership in Y⟨d+⟩Y\langle d^+\rangle, the hypothesis of the same statement for dd, so the descent for aa can use the statement for dd at smaller levels, by induction on the level alone. Applied to the tree of [A] that searches for a Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinal, this gives Theorem 6.9. The descents rest on bounded tests one admissible level up (Lemmas 4.1, 4.2).
