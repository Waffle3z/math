Abstract. For a Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation PP with parameters, a node aa and n≥1n \ge 1, let an ordinal μ\mu be in the class Cn(P,a)C_n(P,a) if it is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting on the classes of the predecessors of aa, the relation of PP being evaluated afresh in LμL_\mu. We show that for every n≥1n \ge 1 the least limit ordinal α>ω\alpha > \omega that lies in the class of every node in the externally well-founded part of every such presentation at α\alpha is the least Πn+1\Pi_{n+1}-reflecting ordinal. The case n=2n = 2 is an earlier paper. The step beyond it is the lower bound for n≥3n \ge 3: a search tree whose promises are kept by choosing witnesses, each choice removing two quantifier blocks, so that only a bounded number of steps must be unwound. For admissible α>ω\alpha > \omega the ranks of these nodes are exactly the ordinals below δα\delta_\alpha, the supremum of the order types of Δ1(Lα)\Delta_1(L_\alpha) well-orderings definable with parameters, and we call the condition Πn\Pi_n-reflection of degree δα\delta_\alpha. At admissible γ>ω\gamma > \omega in which every earlier level is countable, γ\gamma is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting of degree δγ\delta_\gamma exactly when it is Πn+1\Pi_{n+1}-reflecting or Πn\Pi_n-reflecting on the Πn+1\Pi_{n+1}-reflecting ordinals (n≥2n \ge 2). With “admissible limit of” in place of “Πn\Pi_n-reflecting on”, the least ordinal of degree δα\delta_\alpha is the least recursively Mahlo ordinal.

Provenance. The proofs were found and written by AI systems. No human specialist has reviewed them, and novelty is not established. Section 7 states what has been checked and how.

1. Introduction

Main theorem (Theorems 4.3, 4.8 and 4.18). For every n≥1n \ge 1, the least limit ordinal α>ω\alpha > \omega such that α∈Cn(P,a)\alpha \in C_n(P, a) for every Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation PP with parameter in LαL_\alpha and every node a∈Lαa \in L_\alpha in the well-founded part of PP at α\alpha is the least Πn+1\Pi_{n+1}-reflecting ordinal KnK_n. Here a presentation is a formula with a parameter, read as a predecessor relation on LμL_\mu at each level μ\mu; well-foundedness is taken in the universe; and μ∈Cn(P,a)\mu \in C_n(P,a) when μ\mu is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting on Cn(P,c)C_n(P,c) for every predecessor cc of aa in LμL_\mu, the relation being evaluated afresh at each level (Definition 2.1). For n=2n = 2 this is the main theorem of the earlier paper [E][E], The least α+\alpha^+-recursively Mahlo ordinal is the least Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinal, version of 2026-11-22, cited as [E, Theorem B]; we use its conventions and cite its items as [E, Lemma 4.1]. That KnK_n has the property is the known upper-bound argument (Theorem 4.3). The step beyond [E][E] is the lower bound for n≥3n \ge 3 (Section 4.3): the search tree of [E][E] must now keep promises with more than two quantifier blocks, and does so by nesting them. For n=1n = 1 the lower bound is elementary (Section 4.2).

For admissible α>ω\alpha > \omega the ranks of the nodes in the condition are exactly the ordinals below δα\delta_\alpha, the supremum of the order types of Δ1(Lα)\Delta_1(L_\alpha) well-orderings (Theorem 3.1), and we call the condition Πn\Pi_n-reflection of degree δα\delta_\alpha. The name records only which ranks are available at α\alpha: the class of a node is not determined by its rank at α\alpha, because it depends on how the relation is interpreted at the smaller levels (Examples 4.7 and 5.10). When α\alpha is countable, locally countable and below the least Σ11\Sigma^1_1-reflecting ordinal, δα=α+\delta_\alpha = \alpha^+ (Theorem 3.2), which is why [E][E] calls the case n=2n = 2 “α+\alpha^+-recursively Mahlo”.

Characterisation (Theorem 4.21). Let n≥2n \ge 2, and let γ>ω\gamma > \omega be admissible with every LηL_\eta, η<γ\eta < \gamma, countable in LγL_\gamma. Then γ\gamma is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting of degree δγ\delta_\gamma if and only if γ\gamma is Πn+1\Pi_{n+1}-reflecting or Πn\Pi_n-reflecting on the Πn+1\Pi_{n+1}-reflecting ordinals. The countability hypothesis holds at every admissible γ\gamma up to the least ordinal that is Πm\Pi_m-reflecting for every mm, and fails at large admissible γ\gamma. For n=2n = 2 this is [E, Theorem C].

Variants. Each operation on classes below has a next level: for “limit of”, which is Π1\Pi_1-reflection on a class, it is Π2\Pi_2-reflection, that is, admissibility; for “Πn\Pi_n-reflecting on” it is Πn+1\Pi_{n+1}-reflection, so that for this family the least point advances from Πn\Pi_n to Πn+1\Pi_{n+1}; for “admissible limit of” it is Π2\Pi_2-reflection on the admissible ordinals, that is, recursive Mahloness. In each case the least ordinal of degree δα\delta_\alpha is the least ordinal of the next level. In each stated range the ordinals of that degree form the class B∪O(B)B \cup O(B), where BB is the class of the next level and O(B)O(B) is the result of applying the operation to BB; the exceptions, the members not in BB, are those of O(B)\BO(B) \setminus B.

  • Limit of (n=1n = 1). Least element ω1CK\omega_1^{CK} (Theorem 4.8). For limits γ\gamma such that LγL_\gamma contains a well-ordering of ω\omega of type at least the supremum of ω\omega and the admissible ordinals below γ\gamma: of degree δγ\delta_\gamma if and only if admissible or a limit of admissibles (Theorem 4.9). Among all limits γ\gamma of degree δγ\delta_\gamma, the least non-admissible one is ωωCK\omega_\omega^{CK} (Corollary 4.11).

  • Πn\Pi_n-reflecting on, n≥2n \ge 2. Least element KnK_n (Theorem 4.18); characterisation above. The least member that is not Πn+1\Pi_{n+1}-reflecting is the least ordinal γ1(n)\gamma_1(n) that is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting on the Πn+1\Pi_{n+1}-reflecting ordinals.

  • Admissible limit of (recursive inaccessibility). Least element MM, the least recursively Mahlo ordinal (Theorem 5.8). For admissible γ\gamma up to the least ordinal Πm\Pi_m-reflecting for every mm: of degree δγ\delta_\gamma if and only if recursively Mahlo or a limit of recursively Mahlo ordinals (Corollary 5.9). The least member that is not recursively Mahlo is the least admissible limit of recursively Mahlo ordinals.

At admissible α\alpha, restricting to Σ1\Sigma_1 linear orderings gives the same notion of Πn\Pi_n-reflection of degree δα\delta_\alpha (Proposition 4.23).

How the proofs go. For the upper bounds, membership in the class of a node is expressed by a sentence, and reflecting that sentence gives the class of every predecessor, by induction on rank. Theorem 4.3 reflects the Πn+1\Pi_{n+1} membership formula of Lemma 4.2. Theorem 5.7(a) reflects a Π2\Pi_2 membership formula onto the admissible ordinals, with a given bound named as a constant. For the lower bounds, a Σ1\Sigma_1 tree searches for a reflecting level, an ordinal of the next level, at or below the level under test, and a descent shows that the level under test is not in the class of its root. For n≥2n \ge 2 the descent passes a Πn\Pi_n sentence down (Section 4.3); for “limit of” a set relation of large rank suffices (Section 4.2); for “admissible limit of” the descent carries one set down to an admissible target (Section 5). Appendix A lists which result uses which.

Nearest results. Arai (Iterating the recursively Mahlo operations, Definition 1.1 and Lemma 1.2) iterates the recursively Mahlo operation along a relation evaluated in the current universe, and shows that a Π3\Pi_3-reflecting universe lies in the class of every n∈ωn \in \omega up to which transfinite induction along a Σ1\Sigma_1 relation on ω\omega holds; his Theorem 1.3 reduces the theory of Π3\Pi_3-reflecting universes proof-theoretically to limits of admissibles with such iterations along one Σ1\Sigma_1 relation. Theorem 4.3 is that argument for every nn, for presentations with parameters and with the well-founded part taken externally; Arai’s Lectures (Lemma 3.33 and Remark 3.34) and later papers (Conservations, Theorem 2.2; Mahlo classes, Section 1) give inclusions of the same kind, with degrees given by ordinals or notation codes. Richter and Aczel (Theorems 1.9–1.11) characterise Π1\Pi_1- and Π2\Pi_2-reflection on a class and show that recursively Mahlo ordinals lie in the iterates of “admissible limit of”; this is the upper bound of Section 5 in another form. Rathjen (Proof theory of reflection, 1993 preprint) shows that every Π3\Pi_3-reflecting κ\kappa is recursively κ\kappa-Mahlo (Section 2) and, for a weakly compact cardinal KK, that the Mahlo classes of degree below εK+1\varepsilon_{K+1}, with degrees evaluated through hulls, are stationary (Theorem 4.12). All of these are upper bounds or proof-theoretic counterparts. We did not find in them a lower bound of the kind proved here, the characterisations, or the rank statement of Theorem 3.1; Aguilera states without proof that δα\delta_\alpha is unchanged if α\alpha-recursively enumerable well-orderings are allowed.

2. Preliminaries

2.1 Conventions taken from [E][E]

[E, Section 2] is used without change. This section adds only the truth predicates Satm\mathrm{Sat}_m for every mm, built as in [E, (C4)], and the reading of operations on codes from the truth table at ω\omega. In brief:

  • LαL_\alpha is the α\alpha-th constructible level. A set is countable in LγL_\gamma if it is empty or the image of a surjection from ω\omega that belongs to LγL_\gamma. A sentence is evaluated in LβL_\beta only if its constants are elements of LβL_\beta.

  • Formula classes. A formula is Πn\Pi_n if it is logically equivalent to one with nn alternating blocks of unbounded quantifiers, the first universal, followed by a Δ0\Delta_0 matrix. In a limit level a block of like quantifiers can be contracted to one quantifier over tuples and a list of constants to one tuple, so at limit levels we write every Πm\Pi_m sentence as ∀x1∃x2⋯xmθ(x→,q)\forall x_1\, \exists x_2 \cdots x_m\, \theta(\vec x, q), with one variable per block and one constant, and a Πj\Pi_j sentence with j<mj < m counts as Πm\Pi_m by vacuous quantifiers. Block contraction is used at limit levels.

  • Certificates. Cert(W,ξ)\mathrm{Cert}(W, \xi) is a Δ0\Delta_0 formula; a certificate through ξ\xi is a set WW with Cert(W,ξ)\mathrm{Cert}(W,\xi). It consists of syntax data and, for each ν≤ξ\nu \le \xi, a set ℒν\mathcal{L}_\nu and a truth table Trν\mathrm{Tr}_\nu; [E][E] writes MνM_\nu for ℒν\mathcal{L}_\nu, and we keep MM for an ordinal. (C1): every certificate is the true one: ℒν=Lν\mathcal{L}_\nu = L_\nu and Trν\mathrm{Tr}_\nu is the satisfaction relation of LνL_\nu. (C2): an admissible LαL_\alpha, α>ω\alpha > \omega, contains certificates through every ξ<α\xi < \alpha. (C3): GG is the Π2\Pi_2 sentence “ω\omega exists, every ordinal has a successor, and every ordinal has a certificate”; it holds at admissible levels above ω\omega, and a nonempty level satisfying it is a limit level above ω\omega (parameter-free tests are given the dummy constant ∅\emptyset). (C4): [E][E] gives Π2\Pi_2 and Π3\Pi_3 truth predicates Sat2\mathrm{Sat}_2, Sat3\mathrm{Sat}_3 for levels satisfying GG; we use Satm\mathrm{Sat}_m for every mm, defined below. (C5): Σ1\Sigma_1 sentences persist from a level to every larger one. (C6): condensation.

  • Order certificates [E, Section 8]: the canonical well-ordering <L<_L is Δ1\Delta_1 over every admissible level above ω\omega.

  • Countability [E, Lemma 7.2]: if γ>ω\gamma > \omega is admissible and some LηL_\eta with η<γ\eta < \gamma is not countable in LγL_\gamma, then some ordinal below γ\gamma is Πm\Pi_m-reflecting for every mm.

Operations on codes. Formula codes are hereditarily finite sets. Every operation on codes used below is primitive recursive: recognising the codes of Πm\Pi_m formulas in the form above and their matrices, the operations dual\mathrm{dual} and tail\mathrm{tail} of Definition 4.13, and the enumerations of tasks in Definitions 4.13 and 5.3. Each such relation is defined over LωL_\omega by a fixed formula, and “the relation holds of given codes” is the bounded condition that the pair of that formula and the codes lies in Trω\mathrm{Tr}_\omega. So these operations are read from the truth table at ω\omega of any certificate through an ordinal ≥ω\ge \omega, exactly as [E][E] reads its enumeration of tasks (end of [E, Section 6.1]); no data are added to certificates.

2.2 Reflection and classes of ordinals

An ordinal α\alpha is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting if every Πn\Pi_n sentence φ(q)\varphi(q) with q∈Lαq \in L_\alpha true in LαL_\alpha is true in some LβL_\beta with β<α\beta < \alpha and q∈Lβq \in L_\beta. For a class XX of ordinals, μ\mu is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting on XX if the same holds with β∈X∩μ\beta \in X \cap \mu. As in [E][E], ordinals called Πn\Pi_n-reflecting or Πn\Pi_n-reflecting on a class are limit ordinals above ω\omega, and reflection targets need not be admissible. A limit α>ω\alpha > \omega is Π2\Pi_2-reflecting if and only if it is admissible (Richter–Aczel, Theorem 1.8). For any ordinal μ\mu, μ+\mu^+ is the least admissible ordinal above μ\mu. An ordinal α\alpha is locally countable if every element of LαL_\alpha is countable in LαL_\alpha.

  • A limit ordinal is nonzero. LimX\mathrm{Lim}\,X is the class of limit ordinals μ\mu such that X∩μX \cap \mu is unbounded in μ\mu; its members are limits of XX.

  • Ad\mathrm{Ad} is the class of admissible ordinals above ω\omega. An ordinal is recursively inaccessible if it is in Ad∩LimAd\mathrm{Ad}\cap \mathrm{Lim}\,\mathrm{Ad}, that is, an admissible limit of admissible ordinals.

  • Mh(ξ)\mathrm{Mh}(\xi) is as in [E, Section 3.1]: Mh(0)\mathrm{Mh}(0) is the class of limits above ω\omega, Mh(ξ+1)\mathrm{Mh}(\xi+1) the class of ordinals Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on Mh(ξ)\mathrm{Mh}(\xi), and Mh(λ)=⋂ξ<λMh(ξ)\mathrm{Mh}(\lambda) = \bigcap_{\xi<\lambda}\mathrm{Mh}(\xi) at limits. So Mh(1)=Ad\mathrm{Mh}(1) = \mathrm{Ad}, and Mh(2)\mathrm{Mh}(2) is the class of recursively Mahlo ordinals, those Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on Ad\mathrm{Ad}. The members of Mh(3)\mathrm{Mh}(3), the ordinals Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on the recursively Mahlo ordinals, are called recursively 1-Mahlo, just as “1-inaccessible” is one step above “inaccessible”.

  • ωkCK\omega_k^{CK}, for 1≤k<ω1 \le k < \omega, is the kk-th admissible ordinal above ω\omega, ω0CK=ω\omega_0^{CK} = \omega, and ωωCK\omega_\omega^{CK} is the least limit of admissible ordinals.

  • AnA_n is the class of Πn+1\Pi_{n+1}-reflecting ordinals, Kn=min⁡AnK_n = \min A_n, and γ1(n)\gamma_1(n) is the least ordinal that is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting on AnA_n. By Richter–Aczel 1.8, A1=AdA_1 = \mathrm{Ad}. [E][E]’s AA, KK, γ1\gamma_1 are A2A_2, K2K_2, γ1(2)\gamma_1(2).

2.3 Presentations and their classes

Presentations are as in [E, Section 3.2]. A presentation is a pair P=(ρ,p)P = (\rho, p) of a formula ρ(x,y,z)\rho(x,y,z) and a set pp; at a limit μ>ω\mu > \omega with p∈Lμp \in L_\mu, cc is a predecessor of aa at μ\mu if Lμ⊨ρ(c,a,p)L_\mu \models \rho(c, a, p), and (c,a)(c,a) is an edge. Elements of LμL_\mu are nodes. PP is a Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation if ρ\rho is Σ1\Sigma_1. The well-founded part at μ\mu is the set of nodes from which there is no infinite descending sequence, taken in the universe, and rankμ(a)\mathrm{rank}_\mu(a) is the rank of aa in the relation at μ\mu. By (C5), an edge of a Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation at μ\mu is an edge at every larger level, so a node in the well-founded part at γ\gamma is in the well-founded part at every smaller limit μ\mu containing it and the parameter, with rankμ(a)≤rankγ(a)\mathrm{rank}_\mu(a) \le \mathrm{rank}_\gamma(a). If ⊲\lhd is a relation that is a set, P⊲P_\lhd is the Δ0\Delta_0 presentation “(x,y)∈z(x,y) \in z” with parameter ⊲\lhd; its edges are the same at every level containing ⊲\lhd, and we call it a set relation.

For a limit α>ω\alpha > \omega, δα\delta_\alpha is the supremum of the order types of well-orderings whose field is a subclass of LαL_\alpha and which are Δ1\Delta_1-definable over LαL_\alpha with parameters (the notation of Aguilera, Section 2). By Theorem 3.1 below, for admissible α>ω\alpha > \omega these order types are exactly the ranks of nodes in the well-founded parts of Σ1\Sigma_1 presentations at α\alpha; this is why the notions of Definition 2.2 are named after δα\delta_\alpha.

Definition 2.1. Let PP be a presentation. By recursion on μ\mu, for all nodes at once:

  • for n≥1n \ge 1, μ∈Cn(P,a)\mu \in C_n(P, a) if and only if μ\mu is a limit ordinal above ω\omega, p,a∈Lμp, a \in L_\mu, and μ\mu is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting on Cn(P,c)C_n(P, c) for every predecessor cc of aa at μ\mu;

  • μ∈CAd(P,a)\mu \in C_{\mathrm{Ad}}(P, a) if and only if μ∈Ad\mu \in \mathrm{Ad}, p,a∈Lμp, a \in L_\mu, and μ∈LimCAd(P,c)\mu \in \mathrm{Lim}\,C_{\mathrm{Ad}}(P, c) for every predecessor cc of aa at μ\mu.

C2C_2 is [E][E]’s class CC [E, Definition 3.1]. A node without predecessors at μ\mu imposes only the first two clauses.

Definition 2.2. Let α>ω\alpha > \omega be a limit ordinal. α\alpha is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting of degree δα\delta_\alpha if α∈Cn(P,a)\alpha \in C_n(P, a) for every Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation PP with p∈Lαp \in L_\alpha and every node a∈Lαa \in L_\alpha in the well-founded part of the relation defined by PP at α\alpha. It is recursively inaccessible of degree δα\delta_\alpha if the same holds with CAdC_{\mathrm{Ad}} in place of CnC_n.

For n=2n = 2 this is [E][E]’s α+\alpha^+-recursively Mahlo. For a set relation and a node of rank ξ\xi the class C2C_2 is Mh(ξ)\mathrm{Mh}(\xi), restricted to the μ\mu containing the parameter and the node [E, Example 3.2(1)]. Again for a set relation, the class CnC_n of a node of rank 0 is the class of limits above ω\omega containing the parameter and the node, and every member of the class of a node of rank 1 is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting; the class CAdC_{\mathrm{Ad}} of a node of rank 0 is Ad\mathrm{Ad}, and that of a node of rank 1 is Ad∩LimAd\mathrm{Ad}\cap \mathrm{Lim}\,\mathrm{Ad}, the recursively inaccessible ordinals, in both cases restricted to the μ\mu containing the parameter and the node. This is why the iteration of “admissible limit of” is called recursive inaccessibility of a degree. For other Σ1\Sigma_1 presentations the class of a node is not determined by its rank (Examples 4.7 and 5.10).

Class certificates. Membership in these classes is computed inside a level by the method of [E, Lemma 5.1, Steps 1–2], which we state once in general. Let Φ(W,ν,x,H)\Phi(W, \nu, x, H) be a Δ0\Delta_0 formula, possibly with set parameters. For a certificate WW through μ\mu and ν≤μ\nu \le \mu, W↾(ν+1)W{\restriction}(\nu+1) is WW cut down to its rows at indices η≤ν\eta \le \nu; it is a certificate through ν\nu. A class certificate for Φ\Phi through μ\mu is a pair (W,Tab)(W, \mathrm{Tab}) of a certificate WW through μ\mu and a function Tab\mathrm{Tab} on μ+1\mu + 1 with Tab(ν)⊆ℒν\mathrm{Tab}(\nu) \subseteq \mathcal{L}_\nu (written DD in [E]), such that for every ν≤μ\nu \le \mu and x∈ℒνx \in \mathcal{L}_\nu: x∈Tab(ν)x \in \mathrm{Tab}(\nu) if and only if Φ(W↾(ν+1),ν,x,Tab↾ν)\Phi(W{\restriction}(\nu+1), \nu, x, \mathrm{Tab}{\restriction}\nu). The restrictions are read as bounded views of the given graphs, since a transitive set containing a graph need not contain its restriction: a quantifier over a restricted graph is relativised to the given graph by its index guard, equality with a set is tested by the two bounded inclusions, and membership in a set by bounded search for an equal element. The pair (W,Tab)(W, \mathrm{Tab}) has a fixed finite tuple shape, and induction on the fixed formula Φ\Phi gives a bounded test on the given data. So being a class certificate is Δ0\Delta_0.

Lemma 2.3. Let Φ\Phi be as above, and let JJ be a class of pairs (ν,x)(\nu, x) with x∈Lνx \in L_\nu; write Jν={x:(ν,x)∈J}J_\nu = \{x : (\nu, x) \in J\}. Suppose that, for the true certificate WW through ν\nu, x∈Jνx \in J_\nu if and only if Φ(W,ν,x,(Jη)η<ν)\Phi(W, \nu, x, (J_\eta)_{\eta < \nu}).

Appendix B: the Lean statements

The Lean file DegreeReflection.lean formalises three abstract statements. It covers abstract facts only: nothing in it mentions constructible levels or definability. Ordinals are either ordinals of Mathlib or, for the descent, a type Pt with a well-founded order. The file copies, unchanged, the definitions Refl and cls of the Lean file of [E][E] (see the appendix of [E][E]): cls holds pred ok μ a says that ok μ a holds and that μ reflects on the class of every predecessor of a at μ, where reflecting on a class means that every test true at μ is true at a smaller member of the class.

Limits along a set relation. Here r is a relation on a type N and θ an ordinal. IsSuccLimit μ says that μ\mu is a limit ordinal, and IsWellFounded.rank is Mathlib’s rank in a well-founded relation.

  cls (fun η β : Ordinal.{u} => η < β) (fun _ => r) (fun μ _ => IsSuccLimit μ ∧ θ < μ) μ a

Kleene–Brouwer. Finite sequences are lists over a type E of entries, compared by lt:

def LeftOf (t u : List E) : Prop :=
  ∃ p x y v w, u = p ++ x :: v ∧ t = p ++ y :: w ∧ lt y x

def KB (t s : List E) : Prop :=
  (∃ w, w ≠ [] ∧ t = s ++ w) ∨ LeftOf lt t s

def KBOn (T : Set (List E)) (t s : List E) : Prop :=
  t ∈ T ∧ s ∈ T ∧ KB lt t s

theorem kbOn_wellFounded (hlt : WellFounded lt)
    (hT : ∀ s w, s ++ w ∈ T → s ∈ T)
    (hwf : WellFounded fun t s : List E => t ∈ T ∧ ∃ x, t = s ++ [x]) :
    WellFounded (KBOn lt T)

theorem rank_le_rank_kb (hlt : WellFounded lt) (hT : ∀ s w, s ++ w ∈ T → s ∈ T)
    (hstep : ∀ s x, s ++ [x] ∈ T → r (f (s ++ [x])) (f s))
    (hcover : ∀ s c, s ∈ T → r c (f s) → ∃ x, s ++ [x] ∈ T ∧ f (s ++ [x]) = c) :
    ∃ hKB : WellFounded (KBOn lt T),
      ∀ s, s ∈ T → IsWellFounded.rank r (f s) ≤ (hKB.apply s).rank

In the proof of Theorem 3.1, T is the set of histories, f s the end of the history s (so f [] is the node aa), lt the canonical well-ordering <L<_L of the entries, and r the predecessor relation restricted to the well-founded part at α\alpha, which is well-founded. Hypotheses hstep and hcover say that a one-step extension of a history ends at a predecessor of its end, and that every predecessor of the end of a history is the end of such an extension; the latter holds because its witness is in LαL_\alpha. Every history other than the empty one comes before it. What is not formal is the rest of the argument: that the ordering is linear, so that the rank of the empty history is the order type of the histories before it, that it is Δ1\Delta_1, and the bound by δα\delta_\alpha.
