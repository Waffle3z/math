Abstract. For an admissible ordinal α\alpha, let δα\delta_\alpha be Aguilera’s supremum of the order types of well-orderings Δ1\Delta_1-definable over LαL_\alpha with parameters. An ordinal α\alpha is recursively inaccessible of degree δα\delta_\alpha if it lies in every class obtained by iterating “admissible limit of” along a node in the well-founded part of a Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation at α\alpha; degree δαk+2\delta_\alpha^{k+2} iterates the passage to degree δαk+1\delta_\alpha^{k+1} in the same way. The recursively 00-Mahlo ordinals are the recursively Mahlo ones, and the recursively (k+1)(k+1)-Mahlo ordinals are those Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on the recursively kk-Mahlo ordinals. Up to and including the least (+1)-stable ordinal, the admissible ordinals of degree δαk+1\delta_\alpha^{k+1} are exactly the recursively kk-Mahlo ordinals and their admissible limits, so the least of them is the least recursively kk-Mahlo ordinal. The correspondence stops at the first limit: the least ordinal of degree δαω\delta_\alpha^\omega is below the least recursively ω\omega-Mahlo ordinal.

Provenance. The proofs were found and written by AI systems. No human specialist has reviewed them, nothing is formalised in Lean, and novelty is not established. Section 9 states what has been checked.

1. Introduction

We build on [A] and [E] (see the references) and use their conventions and numbering, citing their items as [A, Theorem 5.7] or [E, (C2)]. [A] shows that the least ordinal α\alpha that is recursively inaccessible of degree δα\delta_\alpha is the least recursively Mahlo ordinal, and that up to and including the least (+1)-stable ordinal the admissible ordinals of that degree are the recursively Mahlo ordinals and their admissible limits [A, Corollary 5.9]. Its third open question asks what happens at higher degrees.

Degrees above δα\delta_\alpha. At admissible α>ω\alpha > \omega, the ranks of nodes of Σ1\Sigma_1 presentations are exactly the ordinals below δα\delta_\alpha [A, Theorem 3.1], so higher degrees cannot be ranks of such nodes; the names below record how a class is built, as in ordinal arithmetic, not a rank. Degree δα+1\delta_\alpha + 1 is “admissible and a limit of the class of degree δα\delta_\alpha”. Degree δα⋅2\delta_\alpha \cdot 2 is δα\delta_\alpha further steps of “admissible limit of” over the class of degree δα\delta_\alpha. Degree δαk+2\delta_\alpha^{k+2} asks, for every well-founded node of every Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation, for membership in the iteration along that node of the passage to degree δαk+1\delta_\alpha^{k+1} (Definitions 3.1–3.4).

The main result is the following.

Theorem 7.2 (main theorem). Let k≥0k \ge 0. An admissible ordinal γ\gamma that is at most the least (+1)-stable ordinal is recursively inaccessible of degree δγk+1\delta_\gamma^{k+1} if and only if γ\gamma is recursively kk-Mahlo or a limit of recursively kk-Mahlo ordinals. The least ordinal α\alpha that is recursively inaccessible of degree δαk+1\delta_\alpha^{k+1} is the least recursively kk-Mahlo ordinal.

Recursive kk-Mahloness is defined in Section 2.3. For k=0k = 0 this is [A, Corollary 5.9]; for k=1k = 1, degree δα2\delta_\alpha^2 gives the least recursively 1-Mahlo ordinal. The theorem is the case X=AdX = \mathrm{Ad} of Theorem 7.1, which describes the classes X[k]X[k] of degree δk\delta^k over any base class XX built in Section 3: at admissible γ\gamma at most the least (+1)-stable ordinal, X[k]X[k] consists of the members and admissible limits of the ordinals in LimX\mathrm{Lim}\,X that are admissible if k=0k = 0 and recursively (k−1)(k-1)-Mahlo if k≥1k \ge 1.

Further results (Section 7).

  • Degrees δα+1\delta_\alpha + 1 and δα⋅2\delta_\alpha \cdot 2 (Corollary 7.3). The least ordinal of degree δα+1\delta_\alpha + 1 is the least admissible limit of recursively Mahlo ordinals. The least ordinal of degree δα⋅2\delta_\alpha \cdot 2 is the least recursively Mahlo limit of recursively Mahlo ordinals, which is below the least recursively 1-Mahlo ordinal.

  • Degree δαω\delta_\alpha^\omega (Proposition 7.4). For admissible γ\gamma at most the least (+1)-stable ordinal, degree δγω\delta_\gamma^\omega holds exactly when γ\gamma is a limit of recursively kk-Mahlo ordinals for every k<ωk < \omega. Its least element, the least admissible ordinal with this property, is below the least recursively ω\omega-Mahlo ordinal.

Section 8 says where the statements fail: at exponent 00, at degree δα⋅2\delta_\alpha \cdot 2, at exponent ω\omega, and for step classes tested with a parameter.

How the proof goes. The upper bound (Theorem 5.2) is the argument of [A, Theorem 5.7(a)] one level up: a member of LimX\mathrm{Lim}\,X that is admissible if k=0k = 0 and recursively (k−1)(k-1)-Mahlo if k≥1k \ge 1 reflects the Π2\Pi_2 sentences saying that the classes required of it are unbounded, and so lies in X[k]X[k]. The lower bound (Theorem 6.5) is by induction on kk for all base classes at once. If no recursively kk-Mahlo member of LimX\mathrm{Lim}\,X lies in (ℓ,γ](\ell, \gamma], a Σ1\Sigma_1 tree searching for such a point is well-founded at γ\gamma. The class X[k+1]X[k+1] asks for membership in the iteration of Y↦Y[k]Y \mapsto Y[k] along this tree; applying the induction hypothesis to the base classes of that iteration, a least-counterexample descent excludes γ\gamma. This needs membership in every class X[k]X[k] to be decided by a bounded test on the constructible levels up to the next admissible ordinal (Lemma 4.4), because those base classes must be tested inside the Σ1\Sigma_1 tree. Appendix A lists which result uses which.

Nearest results. Richter and Aczel (Theorems 1.9–1.11) show that recursively Mahlo ordinals lie in the iterates of “admissible limit of” considered there; [A, Theorem 5.7(a)] gives the upper bound for nodes in the externally well-founded part of Σ1\Sigma_1 presentations, which is the upper inclusion of Theorem 7.2 at k=0k = 0, obtained here from Theorem 5.2(A) with X=AdX = \mathrm{Ad} and c=1c = 1. Iterations of the Mahlo operation along orderings, with ordinals or notations as indices, appear in Arai, Lectures on Ordinal Analysis (Section 4, one level higher, inside Π3\Pi_3-reflection) and Iterating the recursively Mahlo operations (Sections 2–3), and in Rathjen, Proof theory of reflection (Remark 4.9, for cardinals); we cite them for comparison only. These works give iterations and upper bounds. We did not find in them degrees beyond δα\delta_\alpha defined through Σ1\Sigma_1 presentations, a lower bound of the kind proved here, or the failure at ω\omega.

Take X=AdX = \mathrm{Ad} and k+1k + 1 in place of kk in Theorem 7.1. Since Π2k+2⊆LimAd\Pi_2^{k+2} \subseteq \mathrm{Lim}\,\mathrm{Ad} (Lemma 2.1), R=Π2k+2R = \Pi_2^{k+2}. By Lemma 2.1(b) the least element of Π2k+2\Pi_2^{k+2} exists and is below π3\pi_3, which is at most the least (+1)-stable ordinal, so it is in Ad[k+1]\mathrm{Ad}[k+1]. Every member of Ad[k+1]\mathrm{Ad}[k+1] is admissible, and one that is at most the least (+1)-stable ordinal is in Π2k+2∪LimΠ2k+2\Pi_2^{k+2} \cup \mathrm{Lim}\, \Pi_2^{k+2}, so it is at least the least element of Π2k+2\Pi_2^{k+2}. ◻

Corollary 7.3 (degrees δα+1\delta_\alpha + 1 and δα⋅2\delta_\alpha \cdot 2).

  • The least ordinal recursively inaccessible of degree δα⋅2\delta_\alpha \cdot 2 is the least recursively Mahlo limit of recursively Mahlo ordinals, and it is below the least recursively 1-Mahlo ordinal.

Proof.

(a) follows, since the ordinals of degree δα+1\delta_\alpha + 1 are those in Ad∩LimD\mathrm{Ad}\cap \mathrm{Lim}\,D.

Proposition 7.4 (degree δαω\delta_\alpha^\omega). The least ordinal of degree δαω\delta_\alpha^\omega is the least admissible ordinal that is a limit of recursively kk-Mahlo ordinals for every k<ωk < \omega, and it is below mωm_\omega, the least recursively ω\omega-Mahlo ordinal.

Proof.

8. Where the statements fail, and open questions

  • Exponent 00. The pattern of Theorem 7.2 does not extend to exponent 00: Ad[0]=Ad∩LimAd\mathrm{Ad}[0] = \mathrm{Ad}\cap \mathrm{Lim}\,\mathrm{Ad}, whose least element is the least recursively inaccessible ordinal, while the matching class, that of the admissible ordinals, has least element ω1CK\omega_1^{CK}, which is not a limit of admissible ordinals. Theorem 7.1 holds at k=0k = 0, with R=Ad∩LimXR = \mathrm{Ad}\cap \mathrm{Lim}\,X.

  • Degree δα⋅2\delta_\alpha \cdot 2. It does not give recursive 1-Mahloness (Corollary 7.3(b)). By Theorem 7.1 with k=1k = 1 and Lemma 3.5(b), δα\delta_\alpha steps of “admissible limit of” over a base term XX give, at admissible levels up to and including the least (+1)-stable ordinal, the class R∪LimRR \cup \mathrm{Lim}\,R with RR the recursively Mahlo members of LimX\mathrm{Lim}\,X, and so use XX only through LimX\mathrm{Lim}\,X; exponent 22 iterates Y↦Y[1]Y \mapsto Y[1], and its least point is the least recursively 1-Mahlo ordinal by Theorem 7.2.

  • Exponent ω\omega. “The least ordinal of degree δαk+1\delta_\alpha^{k+1} is the least recursively kk-Mahlo ordinal” holds for k<ωk < \omega, and its continuation fails at ω\omega: the least ordinal of degree δαω\delta_\alpha^\omega is below mωm_\omega, the least recursively ω\omega-Mahlo ordinal (Proposition 7.4). At exponent ω\omega the conditions “recursively kk-Mahlo or a limit of recursively kk-Mahlo ordinals” are intersected over kk, while recursive ω\omega-Mahloness intersects the conditions “recursively kk-Mahlo”.

  • Parameters. Theorem 5.2 reflects sentences that mention the tests of Lemmas 4.1 and 4.4, and Lemma 6.2 uses the fixed tests in the edge formula of a Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation with parameter (ℓ,k,tX)(\ell, k, t_X), so the constants of these tests must lie in the levels concerned; this holds because the tests have no parameters and the constants of a term lie below its members (Lemma 3.3(b)). If the class Ad\mathrm{Ad} in Definition 3.1 is replaced by a class tested only with a constant outside the level, the upper bound fails: for the least recursively Mahlo ordinal μ\mu, take the class consisting of μ\mu and an ω\omega-sequence of admissible ordinals cofinal in μ\mu, chosen outside LμL_\mu so that every true Π2\Pi_2 sentence of LμL_\mu holds at one of them; then μ\mu is Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on this class, but no member below μ\mu is a limit of the class, so two steps of “in the class and a limit of” already exclude μ\mu.

Open questions.

  1. Allowing every Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation in place of PωP_\omega to supply the exponent in Definition 3.2 defines, at limits α>ω\alpha > \omega, degree δαδα\delta_\alpha^{\delta_\alpha}. Is its least element the least Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinal π3\pi_3, as the third question of [A] asks? We do not treat this degree here.

  2. What are the least elements for the exponent ω+1\omega + 1 and for exponents given by other nodes of Σ1\Sigma_1 presentations?

  3. Does the forward direction of Theorem 7.2 hold above the least (+1)-stable ordinal? The proof uses that bound only for the countability in Lemma 6.4.
