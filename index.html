The first admissible ordinals

  1. The least α\alpha that is Π1\Pi_1-reflecting of degree δα\delta_\alpha is ω1CK\omega_1^{CK}, the least Π2\Pi_2-reflecting ordinal.

    Written proof, checked by AI Theorem 4.8 (reflection of degree δ)

  2. The least α\alpha that is Π1\Pi_1-reflecting of degree δα\delta_\alpha and not admissible is ωωCK\omega_\omega^{CK}.

    Written proof, checked by AI Corollary 4.11 (reflection of degree δ)

Recursively Mahlo and recursively k-Mahlo ordinals

Up to and including the least (+1)-stable ordinal, the least ordinal that is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting for every finite nn.

  1. The least α\alpha that is recursively inaccessible of degree δα\delta_\alpha is the least recursively Mahlo ordinal.

    Written proof, checked by AI Theorem 5.8 (reflection of degree δ)

  2. An admissible γ\gamma at most the least (+1)-stable ordinal is recursively inaccessible of degree δγ\delta_\gamma if and only if it is recursively Mahlo or a limit of recursively Mahlo ordinals.

    Written proof, checked by AI Corollary 5.9 (reflection of degree δ)

  3. The least ordinal recursively inaccessible of degree δα+1\delta_\alpha + 1 is the least admissible limit of recursively Mahlo ordinals.

    Written proof, checked by AI Corollary 7.3 (higher degrees)

  4. The least ordinal recursively inaccessible of degree δα⋅2\delta_\alpha \cdot 2 is the least recursively Mahlo limit of recursively Mahlo ordinals; it lies below the least recursively 1-Mahlo ordinal.

    Written proof, checked by AI Corollary 7.3 (higher degrees)

  5. For every k≥0k \ge 0, the least α\alpha that is recursively inaccessible of degree δαk+1\delta_\alpha^{k+1} is the least recursively kk-Mahlo ordinal. For example, degree δα2\delta_\alpha^2 gives the least recursively 1-Mahlo ordinal.

    Written proof, checked by AI Theorem 7.2 (higher degrees)

  6. For every k≥0k \ge 0, an admissible γ\gamma at most the least (+1)-stable ordinal is recursively inaccessible of degree δγk+1\delta_\gamma^{k+1} if and only if it is recursively kk-Mahlo or a limit of recursively kk-Mahlo ordinals.

    Written proof, checked by AI Theorem 7.2 (higher degrees)

  7. The least ordinal recursively inaccessible of degree δαω\delta_\alpha^\omega is the least admissible ordinal that is a limit of recursively kk-Mahlo ordinals for every kk. It lies below the least recursively ω\omega-Mahlo ordinal, one that is recursively kk-Mahlo for every kk.

    Written proof, checked by AI Proposition 7.4 (higher degrees)

  8. For every position ξ+1\xi + 1 in a recursive well-ordering, an admissible γ\gamma at most the least (+1)-stable ordinal is recursively inaccessible of degree δγξ+1\delta_\gamma^{\xi+1} if and only if it is recursively ξ\xi-Mahlo or a limit of recursively ξ\xi-Mahlo ordinals. Here recursively (ξ+1)(\xi+1)-Mahlo means Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on the recursively ξ\xi-Mahlo ordinals, and at a limit λ\lambda recursively λ\lambda-Mahlo means recursively ξ\xi-Mahlo for every ξ<λ\xi < \lambda.

    Written proof, checked by AI Theorem 5.6 (degree ladder past ω)

  9. At a limit position λ\lambda in a recursive well-ordering, degree δγλ\delta_\gamma^\lambda holds at an admissible γ\gamma at most the least (+1)-stable ordinal exactly when γ\gamma is a limit of recursively ξ\xi-Mahlo ordinals for every ξ<λ\xi < \lambda. The least such ordinal lies below the least recursively λ\lambda-Mahlo ordinal, and position ω+1\omega + 1 is the only position whose least element is the least recursively ω\omega-Mahlo ordinal.

    Written proof, checked by AI Theorem 5.6, Corollary 5.7 (degree ladder past ω)

Π₃-reflection and higher reflection

  1. Every Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinal is α+\alpha^+-recursively Mahlo, and the least α+\alpha^+-recursively Mahlo ordinal is the least Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinal.

    Written proof, checked by AI Theorem A, Theorem B (greatly Mahlo and Π₃)

  2. The least α+\alpha^+-recursively Mahlo ordinal that is not Π3\Pi_3-reflecting is the least ordinal that is Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on the Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinals.

    Written proof, checked by AI Theorem C (greatly Mahlo and Π₃)

  3. When every Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation may supply the exponent, an admissible γ\gamma at most the least (+1)-stable ordinal is recursively inaccessible of degree δγδγ\delta_\gamma^{\delta_\gamma} if and only if it is Π3\Pi_3-reflecting or a limit of Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinals; the least such ordinal is the least Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinal.

    Written proof, checked by AI Theorem 6.9 (degree ladder past ω)

  4. For every n≥1n \ge 1, every Πn+1\Pi_{n+1}-reflecting κ\kappa is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting of degree δκ\delta_\kappa, and the least α\alpha that is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting of degree δα\delta_\alpha is the least Πn+1\Pi_{n+1}-reflecting ordinal.

    Written proof, checked by AI Theorem 4.3, Theorem 4.8 (n = 1), Theorem 4.18 (n ≥ 2) (reflection of degree δ)

  5. For every n≥2n \ge 2, the least α\alpha that is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting of degree δα\delta_\alpha and not Πn+1\Pi_{n+1}-reflecting is the least ordinal that is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting on the Πn+1\Pi_{n+1}-reflecting ordinals.

    Written proof, checked by AI Theorem 4.21 (reflection of degree δ)

δ and Ξ₁, up to σ and beyond

σ\sigma is the least Σ11\Sigma^1_1-reflecting ordinal; p0>σp_0 > \sigma is defined under Notation.

  1. δα=α+\delta_\alpha = \alpha^+ for every countable, locally countable admissible α<σ\alpha < \sigma, and δσ<σ+\delta_\sigma < \sigma^+ (Gostanian).

    Published result quoted as Theorem 3.2 (reflection of degree δ)

  2. Ξ1(α)=α+\Xi_1(\alpha) = \alpha^+ for every infinite α<σ\alpha < \sigma (Girard).

    Published result quoted in Section 1 (δ and Ξ₁)

  3. δα=Ξ1(α)\delta_\alpha = \Xi_1(\alpha) for every infinite α≤σ\alpha \le \sigma, and δα=σ+\delta_\alpha = \sigma^+ for σ<α<σ+\sigma < \alpha < \sigma^+. So the least infinite α\alpha with δα≠Ξ1(α)\delta_\alpha \ne \Xi_1(\alpha) is σ+1\sigma + 1, which is not a closure point of the recursive dilators.

    Written proof, checked by AI Proposition 8 (δ and Ξ₁)

  4. Among the closure points of the recursive dilators, δ\delta and Ξ1\Xi_1 first differ at d=Ξ1(σ2)d = \Xi_1(\sigma^2), where σ2\sigma^2 is the least ordinal that is Σ11\Sigma^1_1-reflecting on the Σ11\Sigma^1_1-reflecting ordinals (Aguilera’s ordinal of that name): δκ=Ξ1(κ)\delta_\kappa = \Xi_1(\kappa) at every infinite closure point κ<d\kappa < d, while δd0=Ξ1(d)<(σ2)+=δd\delta^0_d = \Xi_1(d) < (\sigma^2)^+ = \delta_d. So at dd the parameter-free version still agrees with Ξ1\Xi_1.

    Written proof, checked by AI Theorem 7, Remark 11.11 (δ and Ξ₁)

  5. Ξ1′(α)=δα0\Xi'_1(\alpha) = \delta^0_\alpha for every infinite α\alpha: the predilator form of Ξ1\Xi_1 is the parameter-free Σ1\Sigma_1 form of δ\delta.

    Written proof, checked by AI Corollary 5 (δ and Ξ₁)

  6. Ξ1′(α)=Ξ1(α)\Xi'_1(\alpha) = \Xi_1(\alpha) for every infinite α<p0\alpha < p_0, and the two first differ at p0p_0.

    Written proof, checked by AI Theorem 1 (δ and Ξ₁)

  7. Let γ=Ξ1(p0)\gamma = \Xi_1(p_0). For p0≤x<γp_0 \le x < \gamma, Ξ1(x)=γ<Ξ1′(x)=Ξ1(γ)<p0+\Xi_1(x) = \gamma < \Xi'_1(x) = \Xi_1(\gamma) < p_0^+; at γ\gamma the two agree again.

    Written proof, checked by AI Theorem 1 (δ and Ξ₁)

  8. p0p_0 is the least ordinal dd such that some larger ordinal belongs to every Σ11\Sigma^1_1 class containing dd.

    Written proof, checked by AI Theorem 3 (δ and Ξ₁)

Notation

α+\alpha^+
The least admissible ordinal above α\alpha.
recursively kk-Mahlo, ω\omega-Mahlo
Recursively Mahlo means Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on the admissible ordinals, and recursively (k+1)(k+1)-Mahlo means Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on the recursively kk-Mahlo ordinals; recursively 0-Mahlo is recursively Mahlo. Recursively ω\omega-Mahlo means recursively kk-Mahlo for every kk. Section 2 (higher degrees)
δα\delta_\alpha
The supremum of the order types of the well-orderings of subsets of LαL_\alpha that are Δ1\Delta_1-definable over LαL_\alpha with parameters, modelled on Aguilera’s δα\delta_\alpha for admissible α\alpha; Aguilera calls α\alpha Gandy when his δα=α+\delta_\alpha = \alpha^+. δα0\delta^0_\alpha is the same supremum for Σ1\Sigma_1 definitions without parameters. Section 1.1 (δ and Ξ₁)
degree δα\delta_\alpha
α\alpha is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting of degree δα\delta_\alpha if it lies in the Πn\Pi_n-reflection class of every node in the well-founded part of every Σ1\Sigma_1-definable relation at α\alpha, the class of a node being built from those of its predecessors; recursively inaccessible of degree δα\delta_\alpha uses “admissible limit of” in place of Πn\Pi_n-reflection. The degrees δα+1\delta_\alpha + 1, δα⋅2\delta_\alpha \cdot 2 and δαk+1\delta_\alpha^{k+1} iterate this. Definition 2.2 (reflection of degree δ); Definition 3.1, Definition 3.4 (higher degrees); Definition 3.3 (degree ladder past ω)
α+\alpha^+-recursively Mahlo
For every Σ1\Sigma_1 presentation PP with parameter in LαL_\alpha and every node aa in the well-founded part of the relation it defines at α\alpha, α\alpha lies in the class C(P,a)C(P,a): the limits μ>ω\mu > \omega that are Π2\Pi_2-reflecting on C(P,c)C(P,c) for every predecessor cc of aa. A recursive analogue of greatly Mahlo. Definition 3.3 (greatly Mahlo and Π₃)
Ξ1(α)\Xi_1(\alpha), Ξ1′(α)\Xi'_1(\alpha)
The supremum of the order types |D(α)||D(\alpha)| over the recursive dilators DD (Girard’s notation), and the supremum of |P(α)||P(\alpha)| over the recursive predilators PP that are well-ordered at α\alpha. Section 1 (δ and Ξ₁)
σ\sigma, λ\lambda, p0p_0
σ\sigma is the least Σ11\Sigma^1_1-reflecting ordinal, which Ressayre (III.10) identifies with the least ordinal that is not β\beta-definable. λ\lambda is the least α\alpha that is uncountable in Lα+L_{\alpha^+}, and p0p_0 is the least ordinal that is not Σ1\Sigma_1-definable from λ\lambda in Lλ+L_{\lambda^+}; σ<p0<λ\sigma < p_0 < \lambda. Section 1.1 (δ and Ξ₁)
(+1)-stable
An ordinal α\alpha is (+1)-stable if Lα≺Σ1Lα+1L_\alpha \prec_{\Sigma_1} L_{\alpha+1}; equivalently, α\alpha is Πn\Pi_n-reflecting for every finite nn (Richter and Aczel). Section 2.2 (reflection of degree δ)

Where the parameter-free δ\delta first exceeds Girard’s Ξ1\Xi_1

Girard's Ξ₁(α) is the supremum of D(α) over the recursive dilators D. Its predilator form Ξ′₁(α), the same supremum over recursive predilators well-ordered at α, is the parameter-free δ: the supremum of the well-orderings Σ₁-definable over L_α without parameters. Ressayre asked where the two differ. They agree at every infinite α below p₀ and first differ at p₀; here λ is the least ordinal that is uncountable in the next admissible set, and p₀ is the least ordinal not Σ₁-definable from λ there (Ressayre's names). The paper also gives a criterion for equality at any α and characterises p₀ as the least ordinal that shares all its Σ¹₁ properties with a larger ordinal. Over all infinite ordinals the parameter version δ_α first differs from Ξ₁(α) at σ+1, but among the closure points of the recursive dilators the first difference is at Ξ₁(σ²), where σ² is the least ordinal Σ¹₁-reflecting on the Σ¹₁-reflecting ordinals.

The least ordinal α\alpha that is recursively inaccessible of degree δαk+1\delta_\alpha^{k+1} is the least recursively kk-Mahlo ordinal

The sequel to the paper on degree δ_α: recursive inaccessibility of higher degrees δ_α^(k+1). Up to and including the least (+1)-stable ordinal, the admissible ordinals of degree δ_α^(k+1) are exactly the recursively k-Mahlo ordinals and their admissible limits, so the least one is the least recursively k-Mahlo ordinal. The match stops at the first limit: the least ordinal of degree δ_α^ω is the least admissible limit of the recursively k-Mahlo ordinals for every finite k, below the least recursively ω-Mahlo ordinal (recursively k-Mahlo for every k).

The least ordinal recursively inaccessible of degree δαδα\delta_\alpha^{\delta_\alpha} is the least Π3\Pi_3-reflecting ordinal

The third paper on degrees: exponents past ω. Up to and including the least (+1)-stable ordinal, degree δ_α^(ξ+1) gives exactly the recursively ξ-Mahlo ordinals and their admissible limits, for every position ξ+1 in a recursive well-ordering; at a limit position λ it gives the admissible ordinals that are limits of recursively ξ-Mahlo ordinals for every ξ < λ. So exponent ω+1 is the first to reach the least recursively ω-Mahlo ordinal. When every Σ₁ presentation may supply the exponent (degree δ_α^δ_α), the least element is the least Π₃-reflecting ordinal, answering the open question of the earlier papers.
